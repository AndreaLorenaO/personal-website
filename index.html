About

My professional journey began in geology, where I learned to observe patterns, gather evidence, interpret data, and look beneath the surface, literally and figuratively. Over nearly a decade of research and teaching in academia, I designed and carried out fieldwork, analyzed complex datasets, published scientific work, and presented findings at conferences. You can explore my published papers on ORCID and Google Scholar.

Alongside my research activities, I held several teaching roles supporting undergraduate students in courses such as Mineralogy, Petrology, and Sedimentology. These experiences strengthened my ability to communicate complex ideas clearly, adapt information to different audiences, mentor others, and collaborate effectively within multidisciplinary teams. Science taught me to approach problems with curiosity, rigor, and persistence, while balancing attention to detail with the ability to see the bigger picture. Many of these skills continue to shape the way I work today.

As my career evolved, I became interested in technology as a way to support my geological work. I had the impression that, as a scientist, learning how to code would be a valuable skill since, many of the early pioneers of computing came from scientific backgrounds. What started as a Java workshop quickly turned into something much bigger. I became fascinated by the logic, creativity, and problem-solving involved in building software. Through backend development, I discovered that the systems thinking, analytical mindset, and structured approach I had cultivated in science translated naturally into technology.

Over time, my curiosity grew beyond writing code. I became increasingly interested in how digital products are conceived, structured, and brought to life, as well as in the people and processes behind them. During my design training, I had the opportunity to work on end-to-end design projects, applying UX and UI principles to transform ideas into tangible solutions. These projects allowed me to explore user needs, define product concepts, map user flows, create wireframes, and develop visual designs. This led me to explore human-centered design, where I found a way to combine technical understanding, systems thinking, and a desire to create meaningful experiences.

Today, I draw on my interdisciplinary background to approach problems from multiple perspectives. I enjoy navigating complexity, connecting ideas across disciplines, and designing solutions that balance people needs, technical feasibility, and broader organizational goals. Feel free to explore my work and the experiences.
